Variance Decomposition

Unlock the geometric intuition behind Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). Understand how we separate random noise from true effects by dissecting the sum of squared differences.

The Fundamental Identity of ANOVA:
$$SS_{\text{Total}} = SS_{\text{Treatment}} + SS_{\text{Error}}$$
The total variation in your data ($SS_{\text{Total}}$) is decomposed into variation due to treatment differences ($SS_{\text{Treatment}}$) and variation due to random error/within-group noise ($SS_{\text{Error}}$).

3. Interactive Degrees of Freedom ($df$)

Degrees of freedom represent the number of values in a calculation that are "free to vary". In ANOVA, we partition these degrees of freedom just like we partition the sums of squares.

How $df$ Partitions in ANOVA

Total Degrees of Freedom ($df_{\text{Total}}$) N - 1

With $N$ total data points, if we fix the grand mean, only $N - 1$ points are free to vary. The last point's value is locked to satisfy the grand mean constraint.

Treatment Degrees of Freedom ($df_{\text{Treatment}}$) k - 1

For $k$ groups, we calculate the variation of the group means around the grand mean. If the grand mean is fixed, only $k - 1$ group means are free to vary.

Error Degrees of Freedom ($df_{\text{Error}}$) N - k

Within each group $j$ of size $n_j$, the point values vary around their own group mean. For group $j$, this gives $n_j - 1$ degrees of freedom. Summing across all $k$ groups: $\sum (n_j - 1) = N - k$.

Degrees of Freedom Identity:
$$df_{\text{Total}} = df_{\text{Treatment}} + df_{\text{Error}}$$
$$\text{e.g., } 11 = 2 + 9$$
